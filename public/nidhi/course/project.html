The portfolio piece · after Excel 1.4

Build the HR Recruitment & Attrition dashboard yourself.

A Cursor-built workbook already exists with this exact shape. That file is the spec, not the portfolio. In an interview you have to open a file you can defend cell by cell. This chapter is the build: every sheet, every formula, why it is there, and how to type it. You repeat the same steps on your Ferra workbooks on your own laptop. Real candidate names never come onto this website.

Two files, two rules. On this page you practise on a tiny invented plant — six requisitions, three roles, three months. On your laptop you rebuild the full Ferra dashboard from HR Dashboard - Rec-Nidhi and the Outlook calendar export. You do not paste those names, phone numbers, salaries or CVs into this course, into a public AI tool, or into a portfolio you email. The interviewer sees the structure and the formulas. The people stay at work.

What “done” looks like

The workbook you are copying

The finished file is an Excel workbook in two colours of tab. Navy tabs are what a director reads. Grey tabs are the source. One hidden sheet in the middle does the matching so the director's page can be all formulas.

LayerSheetsRule
Source data (grey)Monthly Snapshot, Requisition Tracker, Role Funnel YTD, Monthly Role Funnel, Resignations, Upcoming Joiners, Time to Fill, Interview Log, and the other calendar sheetsCopied from your existing files. One row per fact. Turned into Tables. No charts here.
Filter Engine (hidden)One sheetPick lists, criteria, and a 1/0 flag on every requisition and interview. Nothing a human reads.
Presentation (navy)Executive Summary, Role Explorer, Headcount & Movement, Recruitment Overview, Funnel & Conversions, Source Analysis, Role Performance, Department Analysis, TAT & Aging, Interview Effectiveness, Weekly & Monthly Activity, Attrition Analysis, Insights & Actions, Notes & MethodNo typed numbers. Every card is a formula. Charts sit on the same tables they describe.

Why we built it this way, not as one giant “Dashboard” sheet. A single sheet with everything on it cannot answer “show me CAM in March” without tearing the layout apart. Splitting source from presentation means you can paste a new month into the grey tabs, refresh, and the navy tabs move. The hidden engine is how two drop-downs can drive sixteen cards at once. That is the sentence you give when someone asks why it is not just a PivotTable.

The unglamorous hour that saves the file

Cleaning, before a single chart

The source workbooks were built for reading, not for formulas. Three things always break a dashboard if you skip them.

  1. One spelling per role. The snapshot says Deburrer in one block and Deburer in another. Upcoming Joiners says “SCM -Lead” while the tracker says “Sr. Engineer - SCM”. You make a small sheet called Crosswalk with three columns: Requisition name, Snapshot name, Calendar name. INDEX/MATCH from there. You do not “fix” the source by overwriting history — you map it.
  2. Numbers that look like numbers. Left-aligned days-to-fill are text. =VALUE() or Power Query Change Type, then replace the column. SUM of text is a silent undercount. You already knows this from chapter 1.1.
  3. Totals that actually total. On the real file, several SUM rows stopped short of the last role and the last months, so they under-counted. You rewrite those totals as =SUM(B14:M26) covering the whole block, or you delete the total row and lets your presentation sheet total instead. A wrong total on a source sheet poisons every card above it.

Two more corrections the original build had to make, so you make them on purpose:

  • On Time to Fill, some Actual Days cells pointed at columns that do not exist and showed zero for still-open roles. Blank them. Zero days is a lie; a blank is “not recorded.”
  • Never invent a benchmark, a target, or a “typical manufacturing rate” to fill a gap. If the source cannot support a metric, the cell is an em dash —, not a guess. That is why the finished file looks honest in an interview.

The sheet nobody sees and everybody depends on

Step: build the Filter Engine

Role Explorer has two drop-downs: a role and a month. Every card must follow both. Excel cannot put a drop-down inside SUMIFS directly, so a hidden sheet turns the two picks into criteria the COUNTIFS family understands.

Why these two formulas exist

COUNTIFS has no “ignore this filter” switch. The trick is to feed it a criterion that matches everything when the user picks All.

PickWhat we store in a cellWhy
Role = All roles"*"COUNTIF(range,"*") matches every non-blank. The filter is off.
Role = CAM Programmer"CAM Programmer"Only that role matches.
Month = All months">0"Month numbers are 1–12. Greater than zero means every month.
Month = Mar"=3"Built with "="&INDEX(...) so March is the number 3, not the text "Mar".
 
=IF(I2="All roles","*",I2)
=IF(I4="All months",">0","="&IFERROR(INDEX(F3:F14,MATCH(I4,E3:E14,0)),0))

Then, on every requisition row, a match flag:

=IF(AND(COUNTIF(Reqs[@Position], Engine!I3)>0, COUNTIF(K2, Engine!I5)>0), 1, 0)

And a running rank of the matches, so the presentation sheet can pull “the 1st matching row, the 2nd…” with INDEX/MATCH:

=IF(L2=1, SUM($L$2:L2), "")

That running SUM is why the rank is 1, 2, 3 on the matching rows and blank on the rest. MATCH(1, ranks, 0) then finds the first match, MATCH(2, ranks, 0) the second.

Try it on the practice plant

Three roles, six requisitions. Column G is the month-number helper — the real file uses =MONTH([@Raised]). J2 is the role pick, J4 the month pick. Gold cells: the two criteria, then the match flags. Fill the flags down after the first one works.

The page a director opens first

Step: Executive Summary

Eight cards across the top, then a role-and-month pair of drop-downs, then a monthly movement table with a combo chart (joiners and exits as columns, headcount as a line). Nothing on this sheet is typed.

CardFormula, in EnglishWhy this source, not another
HeadcountThe latest reported closing headcount on Monthly SnapshotA stock. Summing months would triple-count people who stayed.
Joiners YTD=SUM(Snap[Joiners]) across reported monthsA flow. Summing is correct.
Exits YTD=SUM(Snap[Exits])Same.
Requisitions raisedSnapshot “open requisitions” row, summedThe headline the source itself presents. Role Funnel YTD will not always agree — Notes & Method will say so, not a silent average of the two.
Positions closedSnapshot closed rowSame honesty rule.
Offers released=SUM(FunnelYTD[Offers])Role Funnel YTD is the year-to-date role list.
Candidates sourced=SUM(FunnelYTD[Sourced])Same sheet, so yield is apples to apples.
Sourced to joined yield=IFERROR(SUM(FunnelYTD[Joined])/SUM(FunnelYTD[Sourced]),"—")DIVIDE-by-hand with a dash, never a #DIV/0! on a director's page.

The four cards under the drop-downs — open positions, closed, joinees, upcoming — cannot use those summary rows, because the summary rows are not split by role and month. Flatten the snapshot role blocks on the Filter Engine: one row per role per month, three value columns, then:

=SUMIFS(Engine[Open], Engine[Role], Engine!I12, Engine[MonthNum], Engine!I14)

where I12 and I14 are the same All-versus-specific criteria trick. Upcoming joiners have a future date, not a month in the reporting period, so that card follows the role only.

The disagreement you must not hide. On the real Ferra file, the snapshot's summary rows and the role blocks do not always add to the same number. The cards at the top read the summary. The drop-down cards read the blocks. Notes & Method records the gap. Averaging them would be a lie, and an interviewer who knows the plant would catch it.

Monthly movement table: Month, Closing headcount, Raised, Closed, Joiners, Exits, Net change =Joiners-Exits, Serving notice, To be joined. Combo chart: clustered columns on Joiners and Exits, line on Closing headcount on a secondary axis. Title in a sentence: “Joiners versus exits, headcount as the line.”

The page that earns the drop-downs

Step: Role Explorer

This is the sheet you screen-shares. Two drop-downs at the top. Sixteen cards. A register of matching requisitions. A pipeline. Offer outcomes. Days-to-fill. The latest interviews. Every number recalculates when either pick changes.

How a card follows both drop-downs

Openings raised, from the tracker:

=SUMIFS(Reqs[Openings], Reqs[Position], Engine!I3, Engine[ReqMonth], Engine!I5)

I3 is * or a role. I5 is >0 or =3. ReqMonth is a helper column =MONTH([@Raised]).

Closed requisitions need a third criterion:

=COUNTIFS(Reqs[Position], Engine!I3, Engine[ReqMonth], Engine!I5, Reqs[Status], "Close")

Average actual days, ignoring blanks:

=IFERROR(AVERAGEIFS(Reqs[ActualDays], Reqs[Position], Engine!I3, Engine[ReqMonth], Engine!I5, Reqs[ActualDays], ">=0"), "—")

Some cards cannot follow the month, because Role Funnel YTD is year-to-date only. Those cards — open at latest month, actually joined, awaiting joining, declines, drop-offs — SUMIFS on the role criterion alone. The footnote on the card says so. Hiding that would make March look like a year.

Pulling the matching rows onto the page

The register uses the rank column from the engine. For the first matching requisition's source channel:

=IFERROR(INDEX(Reqs[Source], MATCH(1, Engine[ReqRank], 0)), "")

Copy down with MATCH(2), MATCH(3)… Over-or-under target is a local formula on the pulled days:

=IF(OR(E10="", F10=""), "", F10-E10)

The pipeline table

Nine stages from the tracker: Sourced, Screening, Sent to HM, Shortlisted, 1st, 2nd, Final, Selected, Offers. Each row is a SUMIFS like the cards. Two extra columns:

=IF($B$12=0, "—", B12/$B$12)     /* share of sourced */
=IF(B11=0, "—", B12/B11)          /* step conversion */

Bar chart on the Candidates column. Title: “Pipeline for the current selection.”

Interviews, and the calendar name problem

The tracker says “Sr. Engineer - SCM”. The calendar says “SCM”. A COUNTIFS on the raw strings returns zero and looks like nobody interviewed. The engine has a Calendar role column filled from the crosswalk. Empty must become a sentinel (“— no calendar role —”) so All-roles does not accidentally match blanks.

=COUNTIFS(ILog[Role], Engine!I7, Engine[IvMonth], Engine!I5, ILog[Canceled], "No")

Candidates interviewed are counted once per role, not once in the whole log. Two people can share a first name. A global unique count would merge them. On one role they are distinct; across all roles the higher count is the true one. Notes & Method says this in one sentence.

Stocks are not flows

Step: Headcount & Movement

This sheet exists because a director will add joiners and subtract exits and expect headcount to move by that amount — and on the real file, it does not. You report both, side by side, labelled.

Source of hire, not source of applicants

Step: Source Analysis

One table, grouped on the Requisition Tracker's Source column, names reproduced exactly — including ugly combined entries. You do not rename “Naukri / Referral” into two rows unless you can split the underlying requisitions.

For each source:

=COUNTIFS(Reqs[Source], A2)
=SUMIFS(Reqs[Openings], Reqs[Source], A2)
=SUMIFS(Reqs[Sourced], Reqs[Source], A2)
=SUMIFS(Reqs[Offers], Reqs[Source], A2)
=IF(Offers=0,"—",Sourced/Offers)          /* sourced per offer */
=IF(Sourced=0,"—",Offers/Sourced)        /* sourced to offer */
=IFERROR(AVERAGEIFS(Reqs[ActualDays], Reqs[Source], A2, Reqs[ActualDays], ">=0"), "—")

Bar of openings or offers by source, not a pie. The column that matters in the interview is sourced-per-offer: a channel that sends volume without offers is the agency-fee lesson from Eva, measured.

Days from the row, not from TODAY()

Step: TAT & Aging

The tracker stores elapsed days, not a close date. Ageing from =TODAY()-[@Raised] would contradict the source. You use the recorded Actual Days column.

CardFormula
Average target days=AVERAGEIFS(Reqs[TargetDays], Reqs[TargetDays], ">0")
Average actual days=AVERAGEIFS(Reqs[ActualDays], Reqs[ActualDays], ">=0")
Average variance=AVERAGE(IF((Actual>="")*(Target>=""), Actual-Target)) or a helper column =[@ActualDays]-[@TargetDays] then AVERAGEIFS on that
On time / LateCOUNTIFS on the helper: variance ≤ 0 versus > 0
On-time rateOn time / timed requisitions

Delay bands: on or ahead, 1–30 late, 31–60 late, >60 late. Aging of active requisitions only: 0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 91–120, >120. A requisition with no actual days yet sits in the first band, and the footnote says so.

Overrun flag, already on the engine: =IF(AND(ISNUMBER(Target), ISNUMBER(Actual), Actual>Target), 1, 0). Role Explorer's “Ran past target days” is COUNTIFS of match-flag = 1 and overrun = 1.

The calendar is a different grain

Step: Interview Effectiveness

The log is events, not people. One candidate can appear three times. You always say which you are counting.

  • Scheduled = COUNTA of the log.
  • Held = COUNTIF Canceled = "No".
  • Canceled = COUNTIF Canceled = "Yes".
  • Turnout = Held / Scheduled.
  • By round, by mode, by recruiter, by panel member = COUNTIFS on those columns. Panel load is a separate sheet (Interviewer Load) because one event has several names in one cell — you split them in Power Query, or you use the pre-split sheet from the calendar build.

Monthly: COUNTIFS on MONTH(Date). Combo of scheduled versus held. You do not treat a canceled interview as a rejected candidate.

Two exit numbers, both shown

Step: Attrition Analysis

Monthly Snapshot has an exits row. Resignations has named leavers, including some dated after the reporting period. You put both on cards: “Exits reported” and “Leavers named”, and Notes explains the gap.

=COUNTIFS(Leavers[Regrettable], "Regrettable*")
=COUNTIFS(Leavers[Status], "Left")
=AVERAGE(Leavers[TenureYears])
=Exits / OpeningHeadcount        /* monthly attrition */

Breakdowns you actually builds: by department, by reason, by regrettable flag, by tenure band. Replacement ratio for a department = joiners in the roles that department hires, divided by exits from that department. The related roles are listed on the sheet (Production → CNC, setter, deburrer, maintenance, production manager, and so on). Without that list the ratio is theatre.

Chart: columns of exits, line of attrition %. Dual axis, attrition formatted as %.

Ranked evidence, not adjectives

Step: Insights & Actions

This sheet is not a slide of opinions. Each row is a rank with the metric that put it there.

  • Roles performing well — sort Role Funnel YTD by closure rate (Closed / Openings), then by Joined. Top five. Evidence sentence built with TEXTJOIN or typed from the numbers, e.g. “Closed 4 of 5 openings (80%); 3 joined from 40 sourced.”
  • Roles needing focus — sort by sourced per offer, descending. A role with sourced and zero offers bubbles to the top with an em dash, not a huge fake ratio. That is the spec-is-unhireable conversation from chapter 8, with a number.
  • Slow requisitions — largest Actual − Target among timed rows. Name the role, the days, the status.
  • Channels — highest sourced-per-offer and highest offer yield, from Source Analysis. Two rows, opposite stories.
  • Commentary — only what is already written in the Monthly Snapshot notes column, quoted. You do not invent a narrative the source does not support.

The sheet that makes your look senior

Step: Notes & Method

Interviewers who have built a file will skip to this tab. It must exist.

  1. A table of every sheet, its origin file, and what one row means.
  2. The requisition-to-calendar crosswalk, printed.
  3. The upcoming-joiner-to-snapshot crosswalk, printed.
  4. Definitions: opening headcount, overrun, turnout, offer acceptance, overall yield, replacement ratio, the em dash.
  5. Where the source sheets disagree, with both numbers and one sentence of why. You do not pick a winner.
  6. Corrections you applied (totals that were short, broken Actual Days formulas).
  7. What the file cannot show: named candidates against a requisition, hiring-manager SLA, approval chain, notice period on offers, reasons for a backout. If the column is not in the source, it is not on a card.

Then layout: freeze the header on every presentation sheet, landscape, fit to one page wide, navy tab colour, Calibri, three colours, no merged cells in tables, print titles on, a date in the header stating the reporting period. Gridlines off on navy tabs, on on grey tabs.

Two minutes, then stop

How you walk it in an interview

Checklist before you call it done. Fourteen navy tabs, all formulas. Filter Engine hidden, drop-downs working, All-roles and All-months widening the view. Notes & Method filled. No typed KPI. No pie chart. No real candidate's full name on a file that leaves the building. Saved as your name, dated, and you can rebuild the Filter Engine from a blank sheet in twenty minutes.

The em dash is a formula, not decoration. =IF(denom=0,"—",num/denom). A typed “N/A” will not update when next month's export lands. A #DIV/0! on a navy tab is how the file fails the interview before you have spoken.
