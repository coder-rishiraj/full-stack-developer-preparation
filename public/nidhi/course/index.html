How to use the course

Every lesson has the same four parts, in the same order. Do them in order. Skipping to the exercise and guessing teaches nothing.

  1. The concept, in plain English. What the thing is, and the recruiting problem it solves. If a lesson cannot name the problem, it should not exist.
  2. A worked example. The formula, already typed into a live grid on this page, with the answer visible. Click the cell and read the formula in the bar above the grid. Change a number and watch it move.
  3. Your turn. An empty cell with a gold outline and a question. Type the formula. Press Enter. The page tells your whether it is right, and if not, what is wrong with it — not just that it is wrong.
  4. The tick box. At the bottom of each lesson: I can do this without looking. Tick it only when that is true. The bar at the top of the page is the honest measure of how far you have got.

The grids on this page are real but they are not Excel. They run a formula engine written for this course. It understands around ninety functions, tables, dates and dynamic arrays, which is enough for everything taught here. It will not do everything Excel does. The point is to practise the thinking anywhere, including on a phone on a bus — then rebuild the same file in real Excel, where the muscle memory has to live.

One rule, and it does not bend. No real candidate's name, CV, phone number, pay, or interview notes ever goes into a practice file, a portfolio, or a public AI tool. Not anonymised, not "just for practice". Everything in this course uses six invented people. If you want to show a recruiter your dashboard, it shows Asha and Ben.

The practice data

One dataset runs through the Excel, Power BI and metrics chapters: six invented candidates and two invented roles. Small enough to check every answer by hand, awkward enough to be realistic — there is a blank where someone never got screened, an agency fee on a person who never started, and a date pattern that makes time-to-hire and time-to-fill disagree. The Ferra dashboard project uses a separate invented plant so you are not mixing software-hiring practice data with manufacturing requisitions.

The answers, so you can always check yourself. 6 applicants, 5 screened, 4 interviewed, 4 offers, 3 accepts, 2 joiners. Offer acceptance 3/4 = 75%. Of the three who accepted, two actually started, so the joining rate is 67%. Asha's time to hire is 23 days, Dev's is 32, Eva's is 39, so the average across the three accepts is 31.3 days. Both joiners came from LinkedIn — but LinkedIn was also four of the six applicants, and that gap is the entire point of source-of-hire.

The six people, in one sentence each, because you will be asked about them for the rest of the course. Asha applied, moved fast, accepted and started — the clean hire. Ben got an offer and said no, which is a decline. Cara was screened and rejected after. Dev is the second clean hire, slower, from an older requisition. Eva accepted and then never turned up, which is a renege, and the agency fee of €4,000 is owed anyway. Farah applied six weeks ago and has not been screened, which is the one thing on this list that is Nidhi's fault.

Scope

What this course deliberately leaves out

Four months is short. These are real skills that will not move a European TA Partner interview, listed so you can stop feeling guilty about not doing them.

In Excel

VBA and macros, Solver, financial functions, statistical tests beyond a median, SUMPRODUCT, Power Pivot and Power View, and the old Ctrl+Shift+Enter array formulas.

In Power BI

Building paginated reports, Python and R visuals, administering a Fabric tenant, enterprise deployment-pipeline ownership, XMLA/Tabular Editor engineering, and PL-300 exam prep until after your portfolio report exists. You will still learn what RLS, Fabric, Direct Lake, calculation groups and deployment pipelines are, because interviewers ask.

In HR

Another general HR certificate. CIPD and SHRM are real qualifications and the wrong use of these four months. Payroll, Tally, and US employment law.

In AI

Prompt-engineering certificates. One applied course — IBM's Generative AI for HR — after the Excel file and the Power BI report exist. Nothing before that.

If you only has time for four things

The Excel workbook, the Power BI page, Boolean sourcing, and knowing the software roles well enough to run an intake meeting. Those four are what a European Talent Acquisition Partner interview tests that your current CV does not already prove. Everything else in this course makes your better. Those four get your the interview and through it.
