Chapter 10

Say it out loud until it is boring

The course is useless if the answers only exist as text you have read. This chapter is the interview: your story, the twenty questions you will actually get, the salary conversation, and the 20-engineer case. Every drill has a timer. Tick a lesson only after you have said the answer, not after you have read it.

Ninety seconds, then sit down

The true story, in one paragraph

This is "tell me about yourself." It has to match the CV, name the switch you are making, and end on what you want. It does not start in 2019, it does not mention Tally, and it does not apologise for needing a visa.

I have recruited since 2021 — first in education hiring, then at Ferra Aerospace, where I own hiring across engineering, production, quality, CAM, project management and supply chain. I run the intake, source and screen technical profiles, work the hiring managers, negotiate the offer and keep the MIS. I have spent the last months building the part of the job European teams actually test: a recruiting tracker and a Power BI report I can walk through, Boolean for software as well as manufacturing, and the GDPR and permit rules for Ireland, the Netherlands and Germany. I am looking for a Talent Acquisition Partner seat in Dublin first, where I can hire scarce people and show the funnel — not a bulk-hiring coordinator role.

Four parts, one number

STAR, used like an adult

Situation, Task, Action, Result. European interviewers still use it. The failure mode is a five-minute Situation and a Result with no number. Keep Situation to two sentences, make Action the longest part, and put a number or a named decision in the Result. If you cannot name a number you can defend, the Result is the decision you made and what happened next.

PartHow longFor a Ferra story
SituationTwo sentences"We opened a manufacturing-engineer seat in Pune that had already been open for weeks, and the manager's spec listed eight mandatories."
TaskOne sentence"I owned the search and the manager relationship."
ActionMost of the answerIntake, split must/should, mapped two competitor plants, wrote the Boolean, screened, reset the band. Named steps, not "I worked hard."
ResultTwo sentencesA hire you can describe, or an honest miss plus what you changed. No invented time-to-fill.

Amazon was HR operations, not recruiting: US timesheets and employee cases. You may use it as proof you have worked inside a global technology company. You may not use it as a recruiting story. BYJU'S keeps the six-month top-performer line if you can defend it. Internships stay off the headline.

Twenty questions, tapped open

The question bank

Tap a card, say the answer out loud with the timer, then open it. These are the questions a European TA Partner interview actually uses. If you blank, that card is the next drill.

The question that reveals whether you researched

"Why this company, why this country?"

A generic answer about "growth opportunities in Europe" is a rejection. You need one sentence about the company that could not be copied to the next tab, and one sentence about Ireland (or the Netherlands, or Germany) that names the visa path rather than the weather.

The number, then shut up

The salary conversation

You are aiming at €70,000–€80,000 gross in Ireland, or the local equivalent that still meets the permit floor. You never open at €50,000. You compare offers on gross pay, bonus, pension, health insurance, leave, relocation and who pays the visa — not on the headline alone. Take-home is much lower after tax, and you should not pretend you have calculated Irish net pay to the euro unless you have.

The line to paste into your notes: "For a Talent Acquisition Partner role I am looking at €70,000 to €80,000 gross in Ireland, or the local equivalent that meets the work-permit threshold. I am flexible on the mix of base, bonus and relocation, and I need the offer to be sponsorable."

If they push for a current CTC conversion, you do not let an Indian rupee number set a European band. You can share a range in INR if pressed, then return to the European number: "That is not the market I am joining. The relevant figure is the band for this seat in this city."

The question your CV invites

"You have hired manufacturing people. Why should we let you hire software?"

This is the whole point of the 20-engineer project. You do not claim you have already hired twenty backend engineers in Dublin. You claim you know how you would, and you can show the file.

Walk the project without opening the laptop

The 20-engineer case, out loud

A European product company needs 20 engineers in six months: 8 backend, 6 frontend, 3 DevOps, 3 data. Dublin, Amsterdam, Berlin. You have built the project in the plan. This is the ten-minute readout, the same shape as the Power BI walkthrough: numbers, then what you would do on Monday.

You are interviewing them too

Questions you ask at the end

Three is enough. They should tell your whether the seat is real, whether they sponsor, and whether you would be an order-taker. Generic questions about "culture" waste the slot.

  1. "When this role is going well in six months, what has changed in the funnel that you can already see is a problem?"
  2. "Walk me through the last time you sponsored a permit — who owned the file, and how long it took." If they have never done it, that is the answer.
  3. "How does a recruiter here disagree with a hiring manager — what happened the last time the spec was wrong?"

You are ready when

The ninety-second story matches the CV. You can do STAR without saying the letters. You can explain Farah and Eva from the practice file without looking. Extra reps: craft and job labs. Then you go back to the plan and send the next application.
