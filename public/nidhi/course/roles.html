Chapter 6

Roles to recognise

Two kinds of recognition matter. First, the HR job titles you should be applying to — because the right title is half the search. Second, the technical roles you will be recruiting for, well enough to hold a credible conversation with a hiring manager and not waste a candidate's time.

What to search for

Set alerts on the five titles that matter: Talent Acquisition Partner, Talent Insights Analyst, People Analytics Analyst, Recruitment Operations, and TA Analyst. Searching only "HR" or "Recruiter" misses most of the roles that would pay your best, because the analytics-leaning titles rarely contain either word.

Credibility with a hiring manager

The technical roles you will recruit for

You do not need to code. You need to know what each role does, what the adjacent-but-different role is, and one question that proves you understand the distinction. Tap each card.

Reading a job spec without panicking

Technologies, grouped so they make sense

The point is not to learn these. It is to recognise which group a word belongs to, so that an unfamiliar term is "a frontend framework I have not seen" rather than a blank.

What seniority actually means

Levels, and why they matter for pay

LevelRough experienceWhat changes
Junior / Graduate0–2 yearsNeeds supervision and well-defined tasks. Rarely visa-sponsored.
Mid / Engineer2–5 yearsDelivers a feature independently. The highest-volume hiring band.
Senior5–8 yearsOwns a system, mentors, makes design decisions. Usually the minimum for a work-visa salary threshold.
Staff / Principal8+ yearsInfluences across teams without managing people. A genuinely different job, and a hard one to source.
Engineering ManagerVariesManages people. A different candidate pool entirely, and a different assessment. Not a promotion from Staff — a sideways move.

Why a recruiter must get the level right. It sets the salary band, the interview loop, and whether the role clears a visa threshold at all. Sourcing a mid-level engineer for a senior requisition wastes everyone's time; sourcing a senior for a mid-level budget wastes a candidate's goodwill. And in Ireland the €68,911 Critical Skills threshold means a junior role often cannot be sponsored, which is a conversation to have with the hiring manager before sourcing starts rather than after.

The conversation that decides everything

The intake meeting

Thirty minutes with the hiring manager before sourcing begins. Skipping it is the root cause of most failed searches, and running one well is a senior behaviour that can be described in an interview.

  1. "Tell me about the last person who did this well — and the last person who did not work out." The most useful question in recruiting. It gets the real bar, in concrete terms, instead of a wish list.
  2. "Of these eight requirements, which three would you hire someone without?" Forces prioritisation. A spec with eight must-haves has no must-haves.
  3. "What will this person be doing in their first ninety days?" Turns a list of technologies into an actual job you can describe to a candidate.
  4. "Who are they working with, and who do they report to?" Candidates ask this in the first call, every time.
  5. "What is the band, and is there flexibility?" Settle it before sourcing, not after an offer is refused.
  6. "Can we sponsor a visa for this role, and does the salary clear the threshold?" In Europe this is essential and routinely forgotten until a candidate has already been interviewed.
  7. "Who is interviewing, what is each stage for, and how fast can you give feedback?" Agree the process and the feedback SLA up front. Most candidate drop-out is caused by silence.
  8. "Which companies should I be looking at — and which should I avoid?" Managers know their market. This question alone usually halves the sourcing time.

Say this in an interview and it will register: "I will not start sourcing without an intake meeting, and the question I always ask is about the last person who did the job well and the last one who did not. A manager can describe those two people precisely, and that gives me a real bar instead of a list of eight must-haves that are not all must-haves."

Before moving on

You should be able to name five HR job titles worth setting alerts on, explain the difference between a data engineer and a data scientist and between DevOps and backend, place an unfamiliar technology into the right group, state what senior means and why it matters for a visa, and list the three intake questions that change a search. Extra reps: craft and job labs.
